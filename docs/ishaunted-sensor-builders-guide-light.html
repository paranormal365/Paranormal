The short version

The Paranormal Platform turns a phone into an instrument panel: it reads the phone's own magnetometer, microphone, camera and location many times a second, keeps each reading against one clock, and plays a whole night back as one timeline. A sensor you build becomes one more channel on that timeline.

Start with Bluetooth LE

A sensor on its own battery, talking Bluetooth LE. No Apple approval, works with every iPhone the app supports, and the sensor's own electronics stay away from the phone's compass. Recommended for a first instrument.

Wired, for waveforms

An accessory on the phone's USB-C port, through a hub that also charges the phone. Best when the signal is fast — an ELF waveform, wideband sound — and the accessory can present itself as a USB microphone.

What exists today, and what does not. The format your readings travel in, the upload, the timeline and both players exist and are built to take new channels. The phone's side of a new sensor — finding it, pairing, reading its channels, drawing its gauge — is built when the first instrument is ready to test against. This guide is the contract both sides build to.

What the phone will accept

An iPhone is not a USB host an app can program. iOS gives apps no general access to USB devices; an accessory has to be one of the kinds the system already understands. That narrows the wired choices to three, and makes Bluetooth LE the path of least resistance.

Phones with Lightning (iPhone 14 and earlier) reach the same USB audio route through Apple's Lightning to USB 3 Camera Adapter, which also passes charging power. Bluetooth LE works on all of them.

Power from the phone: an iPhone's USB-C port can power a small accessory (Apple quotes 4.5 W for charging other devices). Budget well under that, and expect the phone to cut an accessory that draws too much at connection.

A Bluetooth sensor node

A single-cell lithium battery, a certified radio module, a quiet regulator and the sensors on one bus. Use a pre-certified BLE module (nRF52840 or ESP32-S3 class) rather than a bare radio chip: the module's certification covers the transmitter, which is the expensive part of getting a product to market.

Power

  • Battery life target: five hours or more. The phone's own recording is designed to last an investigation of that length, and a sensor that dies first leaves a gap that reads like silence.
  • Prefer an LDO near magnetic sensors. If a switching regulator is unavoidable, put it at the far end of the board, keep its inductor's loop small, and measure the node's noise floor with the regulator running.
  • Report the battery honestly over the standard Battery service, and the time since power-on: several instruments drift until warm, and the format records both for that reason.

A reading on the wire

The Channels descriptor

Read once at connection. It is what turns channel 3 and an integer into "1.24 µT, ±0.05":

{
  "format": 1,
  "channels": [
    { "id": 1, "name": "emf_x", "unit": "uT", "scale": -2, "accuracy": 0.05, "range": [-800, 800] },
    { "id": 2, "name": "emf_y", "unit": "uT", "scale": -2, "accuracy": 0.05, "range": [-800, 800] },
    { "id": 3, "name": "emf_z", "unit": "uT", "scale": -2, "accuracy": 0.05, "range": [-800, 800] },
    { "id": 4, "name": "temperature", "unit": "degC", "scale": -2, "accuracy": 0.2 },
    { "id": 5, "name": "elf_field", "unit": "uT", "scale": -3 },
    { "id": 6, "name": "elf_frequency", "unit": "Hz", "scale": 0 }
  ],
  "trigger": { "mode": "hybrid", "interval_seconds": 2, "event_description": "field exceeds 2.0 uT above base" }
}

Names follow the format's conventions — lowercase, snake_case, ASCII units (uT, not µT). A channel the platform has never seen is still recorded and kept; it is drawn once someone adds a gauge for it, so a new instrument is never blocked waiting on the app.

Connection settings Apple asks for

  • Connection interval: at least 15 ms. Apple's accessory guidelines set the exact rules (minimums, multiples, latency and supervision timeout) — design to the current edition of Accessory Design Guidelines for Apple Devices, Bluetooth chapter.
  • Advertising: fast (about 20 ms) for the first 30 seconds after power-on or disconnect, then one of the longer intervals Apple lists, to save battery.
  • Packet size: design so the header and two channels fit 20 bytes; if the phone negotiates a larger MTU, send more channels per notification.
  • Pairing: "Just Works" is fine for a measuring instrument; nothing secret crosses this link.

Time is the whole point

An investigation is read by lining things up: the field jumped at the moment the recording caught a knock. That only works if every reading carries the right time. The node keeps its own millisecond counter; the phone works out how that counter maps to its clock.

  • Stamp at the moment of measurement, not when the packet is sent.
  • Never reset the counter during a session. A wrap of a 32-bit millisecond counter takes 49 days; a reset mid-session is what breaks alignment.
  • Buffer through a dropped connection and send the backlog afterwards with its original times, flagged as buffered. A reading that arrives late but correctly timed is worth exactly as much as one that arrived on time.
  • Say how precise you are. The format records a precision for each reading, so a reviewer never reads false accuracy into a timestamp. Aim for better than 50 ms against the phone's clock.

The ELF front end

High voltage. Geiger-Müller tubes run at around 400 V. Build that supply with creepage distances, a bleeder, a current-limited converter and an enclosure that cannot be opened while charged — or use a PIN-diode radiation module and avoid high voltage entirely.

Transmitters. A radar presence sensor, and the node's own radio, are sources of the very fields an EMF channel measures. Let the Control characteristic turn the radar off, and measure the node's own signature so it can be told apart.

Placement and noise

A magnetic instrument mostly measures whatever is closest to it. The phone, the node's battery, its regulator and its radio are all closer than the room.

  • Measure the node's own floor in a quiet place, radio transmitting, regulator running: that number belongs in the Status characteristic and in the instrument's documentation.
  • Calibrate magnetometers per unit — hard-iron offsets and soft-iron scale — and store the result on the node with its date. The phone records it with every session.
  • Baseline is the investigators' job, and the Control characteristic is how they do it: "set base level" on the phone asks every connected sensor to take its own.
  • Mark a pegged sensor as out of range rather than sending its maximum. The format has a field for it, because a clipped maximum read as a measurement is a false finding.

A sensor case for iPhone and iPad

One idea under consideration: a case that plugs into the phone's USB-C port like a hub, with ports along its sides for sensor modules, so an investigator clips on the instruments the night needs. The same idea works for an iPad, with more room and different hazards.

How the case reaches the phone

The modules' bus cannot be handed to iOS as it is; a small processor in the case reads every module and speaks to the phone as one device, by one of the routes under What the phone will accept:

All wired

The case is a USB composite device: a USB network interface for the readings, and optionally a USB audio interface for a waveform channel. One cable, no radio — but the network route must be proven on an iPhone early.

Wired power, Bluetooth data

USB only powers the case and passes charging through; readings go over Bluetooth LE exactly as from a battery node. It depends on nothing iOS might refuse, and the same firmware serves the case and a stand-alone node.

Modules that describe themselves

Each module carries a small EEPROM holding its Channels entry (name, unit, scale, accuracy), its serial and its calibration. The case reads it when a module is plugged in and adds those channels to what it reports, so a new module needs no new case firmware. A suggested port:

The magnetometer in the case

  • The phone is a magnet. Speakers, the vibration motor, the camera's stabilizer and the MagSafe ring all contain permanent magnets. Their steady field is removed by calibrating the case magnetometer with the phone fitted — and recalibrating if the phone changes.
  • What calibration cannot remove is what changes: the speaker playing, the motor buzzing, the phone charging. Record those moments (the phone knows when it is playing sound) so a reviewer can set them aside.
  • An iPad's edges are lined with magnets for the Pencil and the keyboard. Put the magnetometer top center, clear of the edges, and do not design an iPad case that holds a Pencil magnetically.
  • Keep the hub's switching parts in the chin, the modules' current away from the magnetometer corner, and measure the case's floor with every port loaded. If that floor is too high, a fold-out arm puts the magnetometer farther away still.

If the case is 3D printed

Printing is the right way to iterate on a case: a new revision in a day, and every phone model is a new file rather than a new tool. What changes for an investigation case:

PLAFit checks onlyToo brittle for clips, and softens around 55 °C — a dashboard in summer
Iron- or steel-filledNothingNever: it is magnetic, and turns the case into part of every reading
  • Carbon-fiber-filled filaments are not magnetic, so they are safe near the magnetometer, but they are slightly conductive and dull a Bluetooth antenna placed against them. Keep the radio's antenna end in plain plastic.
  • Fit: work from Apple's published dimensional drawings for each phone and iPad (in the Accessory Design Guidelines), not from measuring one phone. Allow about 0.2–0.3 mm clearance per side for the rigid shell and let the TPU bumper do the gripping; print a fit test of just the corners before a whole case.
  • Walls and clips: 2–2.4 mm walls for the shell. Print so layer lines run along a snap-fit clip, not across it, or it snaps at the first fitting.
  • Fasteners: heat-set brass inserts and brass or nylon screws. A steel screw beside the magnetometer is a hard-iron offset that changes when it is tightened.
  • Port contacts: spring-loaded pogo pins usually have steel springs. Use ones with non-magnetic (beryllium copper) springs on any port near the magnetometer, and put a TVS diode on every exposed contact: a plastic case gives static discharge nowhere else to go.
  • No magnets to hold modules on. A mechanical latch or a printed snap does the same job without adding a field.
  • Openings: the phone's microphones, speakers, camera, flash and buttons stay fully open. A case over a microphone muffles every recording made through it — exactly the evidence the platform exists to keep.
  • Heat: a phone recording video for hours runs warm, and plastic insulates it. Leave the back over the phone's hottest area open or vented, and keep the battery bay away from it.

Power

Pass-through charging is what makes the case usable for a whole night: a USB-C input on the chin feeds a power-delivery controller that charges the phone and powers the modules. Without a charger, the modules run from the phone's port, within its small power budget — or from a cell in the case, which also keeps the phone's battery for the recording itself.

From a sample to the player

Inside the session, a reading from your node looks like this — one more key under measurements, beside what the phone measured itself:

{
  "at": "2026-10-31T23:14:07.240Z",
  "precision": "millisecond",
  "sequence": 5812,
  "triggered_by": "event",
  "measurements": {
    "emf":           { "value": 0.62, "unit": "uT", "baseline": 0.31 },
    "probe_emf":     { "value": 1.24, "unit": "uT", "accuracy": 0.05, "baseline": 0.40 },
    "elf_field":     { "value": 0.180, "unit": "uT" },
    "elf_frequency": { "value": 60, "unit": "Hz" },
    "temperature":   { "value": 17.42, "unit": "degC", "accuracy": 0.2 }
  }
}

The rules that make this safe to extend: every number carries a unit; everything but the time is optional; a reader ignores channels it does not know rather than rejecting the file. A new instrument is an additive change — the format's minor version moves, nothing breaks.

Before a prototype comes to a test night

  • Advertises the IsHaunted service and answers Device Information with real names and a firmware version.
  • Channels descriptor reads back valid JSON with a unit on every numeric channel.
  • Sequence numbers never repeat and never skip, except across a genuine loss.
  • Survives walking out of range and back: buffered readings arrive with their original times.
  • Runs five hours from a full charge with the radio connected.
  • Noise floor measured with the radio and regulator running, and written down.
  • Warm-up drift measured over the first 30 minutes after power-on.
  • Reports out-of-range rather than a clipped maximum.
  • Nothing magnetic in the enclosure, and no MagSafe mount on a magnetic sensor.
  • For high voltage: enclosure closed, supply current-limited, bleeder fitted.

What to send us with a prototype

The Channels descriptor, the GATT table as built, a capture of a few minutes of notifications (any BLE sniffer or phone tool), the measured noise floor and battery life, and the calibration procedure. With those, the phone's side can be built and tested against your instrument rather than an idea of it.
