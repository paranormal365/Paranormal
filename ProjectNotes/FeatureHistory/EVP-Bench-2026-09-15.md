# EVP bench, first run — 2026-09-15

Item 242 phase 1. Produced by `tools/EvpLab` (`generate`, then `score`), corpus seed 242, Whisper small (`ggml-small.bin`,
SHA-1 55356645…, matches whisper.cpp's published hash), CPU only. The raw report follows the findings.

## What it found

1. **Whisper did not invent words from steady noise.** 0 of 160 pure-noise windows, 0 of 295 noise candidates from
   the detector's queue, 0 of 55 knocks/footsteps/creaks/thumps. Feared failure did not appear *on synthetic noise*;
   real tape has more structure (voices through walls, radios, mechanical rhythm), so this must be re-run on Ben's
   recordings before it is believed.
2. **Whisper does invent words from sounds shaped like speech that contain no words.** The same voice clips played
   backwards came back as phrases 92% of the time at +6 dB and 58% at 0 dB — "Good boy." (confidence 0.82), "Thank you."
   (0.83), "Hey." (0.95), "See you around.", "Oh my god!". A sound with a voice's cadence and no words is exactly what an
   EVP candidate is, so this is the failure that matters.
3. **Whisper's confidence cannot be the safeguard.** At a 0.8 threshold 17% of reversed clips still showed words, while
   only 3% of real voices were shown and right. Confidence hides real voices faster than it hides invented ones.
4. **Whisper stops understanding voices at about the level of the noise.** Right: 50% at +6 dB, 8% at 0 dB, 0% at −6
   and below (short single phrases, 1.2–1.9 s clips). Today's detector on High still *finds* 8 of 12 voices at −6 dB and
   2 of 12 at −12. So in the faint range where EVPs are claimed, Whisper cannot supply the words.
5. **As a voice/not-voice filter, Whisper is promising but loses the faint ones.** On the High queue it wrote words for
   21 of 39 real voices and for none of 350 non-voices — the queue would shrink from 389 to 21, but the 18 voices it
   dropped are the quiet ones an investigator most wants.
6. **Cost is not the obstacle.** 0.66 s per clip, 0.38 s per second of audio on an M-series CPU; a Windows server will be
   slower but a queue of a few hundred clips is minutes, not hours.

## What it means for the design (backlog 242)

- Word suggestions from Whisper are only defensible on loud candidates, always behind blind review, and never filtered
  by its confidence alone. Showing "what it might say" on a faint candidate would mostly show invention.
- The valuable job is **"does this contain a voice at all"** — and it has to keep the faint voices. Whisper is a weak
  fit for that; a purpose-built voice-activity detector (Silero VAD, ~2 MB, not yet approved for download) is the
  obvious next thing to grade on the same corpus, and the reversed-speech control is the test it must pass.
- Every detector result shown to investigators should come with this bench's numbers for its level band.

## Limits of this run

Text-to-speech voices (cleaner and steadier than people), synthesised backgrounds, one seed, short clips with no
surrounding context, English forced. Each is a knob for the next run, not a reason to doubt the direction.

# Second run, same day: Silero VAD (voice-activity detector)

Ben approved the download (2.3 MB, `src/silero_vad/data/silero_vad.onnx` from github.com/snakers4/silero-vad at master
41f03a954b84, SHA-256 1a153a22…). Same corpus, same seed, same noise windows.

## What it found

1. **Clean on noise and cheap.** 0 of 160 noise windows and 1–10% of knocks, footsteps, creaks and thumps at the middle
   settings (T 0.5, R 96–192 ms); over whole files it raised no false alarms at all. 739 clips took 2.7 s on one thread —
   about 125 ms per minute of audio. It runs on ONNX Runtime, which the API already loads under IIS on Windows.
2. **It needs the level brought up first.** One +6 dB file scored 0.65 as recorded and 1.00 peak-normalised; the bench
   now normalises every clip to −1 dBFS, which the product's clip export already does.
3. **It has the same ceiling as Whisper.** Voices judged present (T 0.5, R 96): 83% at +6 dB, 50% at 0, 25% at −6, none
   at −12 or −18. Off-the-shelf voice models are trained on voices you can hear; buried voices are outside what they know.
4. **Reversed voices are flagged 25–33% of the time** — correct for a VAD (a reversed voice is a human voice sound); the
   invention problem belongs to Whisper's words, not to "is there a voice".

## What it means

- **Silero VAD earns a place now, as a sorting signal rather than a filter:** order the Scan queue by "sounds like a
  voice" so knocks and hiss sink, but never hide a candidate it scores low — at −6 dB and below it misses most real voices.
  In-process in the API is fine on Windows (tiny, fast, single-threaded, existing runtime).
- **The faint range is where training earns its keep.** Both models stop at the noise level; the known-truth generator
  can produce thousands of buried voices at −6 to −18 dB. Fine-tuning a small classifier on those (and the reversed,
  knock, creak and noise controls) is phase 2 — graded on this bench against the numbers above.
- **Whisper under IIS is a different weight class:** ~500 MB model, ~1 GB memory, all cores for ~0.4 s per second of
  audio. It must not run inside the IIS worker that serves requests (app-pool recycles would reload it and scans would
  starve the site); a separate Windows service taking queued jobs, one at a time, is the shape. Whisper.net ships win-x64
  CPU runtimes (with a no-AVX fallback); confirm on the box whether the Visual C++ runtime it needs is installed.
- **Timings above are from an Apple M-series CPU.** `score` runs on Windows as well (only `generate` needs macOS `say`):
  copy `out/corpus` and the models to the server and run it there for the numbers that matter.

# Third run, same day: VoiceNet — a model trained here to hear buried voices

Ben: "I don't care where it runs as long as I can run it on my windows server and allow it to function fully and learn."
He approved PyTorch (CPU) and LibriSpeech test-clean (MD5 32fa31d2…, matches openslr). `tools/EvpLearn` trains a
60,465-parameter spectrogram CNN whose spectrogram is inside the ONNX file (fixed windowed cosine/sine convolution, peak
normalisation, per-band floor removal), so the .NET side only slices seconds. 12 epochs × 500 steps × 64 one-second
examples: 16 minutes on the M5's CPU. Voices: 30 LibriSpeech speakers train, 10 validate (speaker-disjoint).
Examples: background (synthetic families) + a real voice 0.3–1.0 s long at −20 to +10 dB (a quarter reversed), or an
impostor, or nothing; random low-pass and gain for recorder variety. ONNX output matched PyTorch within 8e-7.

## Why this is encouraging and not yet proof

- **The voices are independent** (trained on LibriSpeech readers, graded on synthetic Mac voices) — the voice results
  generalise across very different speech.
- **The backgrounds and impostors are not independent:** both the trainer and this bench synthesise the same families
  (room, hiss, static, rumble; knocks, footsteps, creaks, thumps), in different code. The low false-alarm numbers are
  therefore optimistic. Real room tone, real HVAC, radios, voices through walls and handling noise are the test that
  decides it — Ben's recordings, passed as `--backgrounds` to both halves, with some held back for grading only.
- Reversed voices are flagged 78–85%: correct for "is a voice present", and a reminder that this model does not say
  whether anything was *said*.
