## Before you start

![The new request form](/help/media/requesting-an-investigation/new-request.png)
*Describing what has been happening. Nothing is sent to a group until you choose one.*

**You do not need an account first.** The form opens to anybody: start at
**Request Investigation** and fill it in. The last step asks your name, an email address and a
password, and your account is created from the request when you send it. If you already have an
account, sign in first and those questions are not asked at all.

Everything you type is kept in this browser as you go, so closing the tab does not lose it.

You will be asked to describe what has happened. Write it as plainly as you can, in the order it
happened. Investigators find dates, times and who was present far more useful than adjectives —
"three knocks on the north wall, about 2am, twice last week" is worth more than "terrifying
activity".

## Choosing a group

**Request Investigation** starts a request and is the quickest way in — at the top of the menu
before you sign in, and under **Community** once you have. To look at who is out there first,
**Join a Group** in the same place lists the groups working in your area. You can
send the same request to more than one; each decides independently whether to take it on.

## What happens next

![The My Requests list showing a request and its status](/help/media/requesting-an-investigation/my-requests.png)
*My Requests tracks where each request stands with each group you sent it to.*

1. The group reviews your request. **Under review** means their members are looking at what
   you submitted — including any photos or files you attached — and voting on taking it.
2. If you sent the request to several groups, each of them can review it at the same time.
   **The first group to accept it takes the case**; the others are told it is no longer
   available.
3. When a group accepts, you get a message naming your group — and your contact person, when
   one has been chosen. Your **case** opens under **My Cases**, and its **Messages** tab is
   where you talk to your group from then on.
4. If they decline, you are told, and you can send the request to a different group without
   writing it again.

## Your address

Groups need your address to reach you — that is the point of the request. **Press Verify Address**
on the first step: we place it on the map so we can find the groups that work near you, and the
form will not go on until we have. It is never shown on public pages, and it is not shared with
groups you did not ask.

## What we do with what you tell us

> Your name, address and what you tell us are shared only with the group you send this to. None of
> it is public, and none of it is ever sold.

That is the whole rule. The group that accepts your case sees your details; a group you did not
send the request to never does; nothing here is published unless you and the group later agree to
publish a case, and even then a case can be published under a name of your choosing rather than
your own.

## Confirming your email

Your account is not usable until you open the link we email you and press the button on the page
it takes you to. Until you do, **the group cannot message you** — they can still see your request
and still accept it, and they are shown that your address is not confirmed yet, so they know why
you may be quiet. Open the link as soon as you can.

The page it lands on tells you where your request stands and shows you your **@name** — the handle
other people use to mention you. We choose one from the name you gave, and it does not change.

## If you already had an account

If the email address you typed already belongs to an account here, the request is **not** added to
it straight away, and the form does not tell you either way — a form that said "that email has an
account" would be a way for anybody to discover who has one. Instead we write to that address with
a note about the request and a link. Sign in with that account, open the link, and you can add the
request to your account or discard it if it was not you. Requests wait two weeks.

## Drafts

A request you have not sent is a **draft**. It is yours alone — no group can see it — and it shows
as *Started* rather than *Submitted* on your list. You can pick it up later, or **Delete draft** to
remove it for good. Once a group has received it, deleting is no longer the right idea: use
**Withdraw** instead, which tells the groups it has been taken back.
